The library keeps your club's old newsletters, breeder reports and care sheets, read into text so anyone allowed can search them. Ask it a question above and it answers from the articles, with links to each one. The easiest way to fill it is to let an AI agent do the reading for you.

Let an AI agent do the work

The site doesn't read scans or photos of pages itself. Connect your own AI agent, like Claude or ChatGPT, and it transcribes them, word for word, into the library.

  1. Connect an AI agent to this site. It takes a minute.
  2. Upload your scans with Add articles, or give the agent a folder of them.
  3. Ask it to "work through my articles". It reads every waiting page and saves each article, with its title, author and year.

Other things to ask:

  • "Digitize the scans in this folder into our club's library."
  • "Tidy our club's library: fill in missing titles, authors and years."
  • "What did our club write about hatching killifish eggs?"

Adding articles

  1. Click Add articles.
  2. Drop in your files: PDFs, Word files, photos or scans of pages. A pile of pages from one newsletter is fine; the articles in it are put back together, one per article.
  3. Pick the club whose library it goes in, and who can read it.

Typed files are read straight away. Scans and photos show Waiting to be transcribed until an AI agent reads them.

Who can read it

Everyone on the siteAnyone signed in who uses the library. The usual choice for club articles.
Anyone, and search enginesOpen to the whole web. For articles your club wants found.
Members of its clubOnly the club's members.
Only meOnly you.
{% tip %}Leave out membership lists and anything with members' addresses or phone numbers, and articles reprinted from magazines or books: those aren't the club's to share.{% endtip %}

Fixing mistakes

Whoever added an article, and the club's admins, can click Edit on it to correct the title, author, year, topics or the text itself. Anyone who reads it can click Report a problem when the text is garbled or the species is wrong.

Copyright

When a copyright owner complains about an article, it becomes private: only the person who added it can read it, and it can't be shared again. It also counts as a copyright strike against their account.
